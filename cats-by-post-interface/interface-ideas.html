Small screen.
Same lovely job.

The interface changes its arrangement, rather than shrinking everything. All sizes keep the same names, ordering, and actions.

01

A phone has a proper lower desk.

The room sits above a compact dispatch tray. Arrival rows stay visible; pieces and Send sit within thumb reach. Extra information opens in a sheet. Controls respect the home indicator and safe areas.

02

An iPad gives the room some room.

The dispatch tray becomes a side desk. Postcards form a small spread; the selected place gets a facing page. Touch targets stay at least 44 points, with no hover required.

03

A Mac keeps a comfortable scale.

Use the extra space for the scene, with a bounded side desk instead of stretched buttons. Space sends or stops; arrow keys and Enter operate the board. Show key hints only when a keyboard is in use.

04

A phone on its side needs a new plan.

A narrow side desk preserves board height. The prototype shows that arrangement when space permits. Short screens need a compact tray and expandable arrivals; shrinking the whole HUD would make it unreadable.

05

Colour is a helping hand.

Give coats distinct face markings as well as colour. Label and number each hatch in both the scene and queue. Use text and shape for next, released, puzzled, and delivered states. Keep a high-contrast option.

06

The little rituals have a light touch.

A postcard turns. A delivery gets one satisfying ink stamp. A new cat settles into the album. Reduced motion uses immediate changes and fades; celebrations never delay the next action.

A home for
the whole game.

A place is a postcard. A chapter lives inside that place. Individual deliveries become stamps, while the cat album holds the permanent collection.

The start screen leads with Continue. Daily Post, Night Shift, and Practice have their own clear entrances; the level editor belongs in a quieter Workshop.

These are illustrative screens with sample progress. The existing game has 40 handmade levels. The approved full journey has six places, eight chapters, and 100 levels. Future rooms here are simple destination sketches, not finished game art.
