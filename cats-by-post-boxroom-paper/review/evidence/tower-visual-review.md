The original rejected flatness, roof contradiction and floating-resident appearance are not present in the views listed below.

This is not a claim of identical fidelity to the generated selected Loft or Quay. Those references have softer ambient shading and more irregular handcrafted details. The current actual Office reference is the more useful comparison for the practical implementation: the revised Loft now communicates grain, exposed edges, cavities and carton contacts at normal play scale. Office remains a more varied architectural composition; that does not make every simpler Loft surface a rendering defect.

## Roof and building

The centred ridge and equal-height eaves now describe a roof over the whole footprint. Retaining the complete far pitch plus the near-left panel reads as a cutaway rather than an incomplete shelter on one perimeter. The full left gable, tie and connected king post give the ridge a legible relationship to the walls. The greater height over the maximum board is a consequence of maintaining pitch over the larger footprint; its roof remains readable in the inspected maximum frames.

I explicitly inspected both ridge ends at both size extrema in day and night. At the left end, the verge and ridge meet continuously; the internal collar does not overpaint the exposed slate. At the right end, the square ridge-cap end meets the far rafter, which descends to the rear-wall post. I see no open gap or floating termination in these eight views. This is an image-based statement about these views, not a general proof of arbitrary camera orientations.

The reduction to 0.18-unit slate edges fixes the former heavy louver effect. The far pitch remains strongly foreshortened at this native view and still has visible courses, but the thin seams now read as overlapping slate rather than deep parallel slats. The near-left panel gives enough area to show the tile surfaces and the two rooflight apertures clearly. The rooflight frames retain a slate margin and visible thickness.

The tower masonry continues beneath the room and beyond the lower image edge at the shown fits. Repeated storeys and the lower town establish altitude much more clearly than the former plinth. Sparse warm night panes suggest an inhabited building without lifting the whole facade. Minimum zoom, pan and continuous-tower bounds are being checked separately by the parent/camera agent; these still images cannot establish them.

## Windows, material contacts and residents

The work windows have visible side reveals and projecting sills. The rooflight panes sit within frames rather than on top of an unbroken slate image. Their broad pale daylight reflection remains stylized, but the assembly has depth. Natural roof overlap is present without destroying the principal work-window views in the inspected captures.

Carton faces carry kraft grain, tape/wear and darker lower contacts. Stacks separate through top/front value changes and narrow seams. The most useful comparison locations are the three taller columns beside the stair/door, the rear corner bank below the ridge and the stacked pads supporting the left cats. These now read as stacked objects rather than a single uniformly coloured extrusion. The wood floor has separate plank courses and directional grain, and tower brick has both mortar structure and face variation. These materials are restrained at fit rather than noisily textured.

In the actual Daily/Night frames, the default residents sit on their carton/pad surfaces. In the integrated eight-coat day/night fixtures, the visible heads remain legible under the retained roof and the bodies meet their supports. I see no apparent hover gap in these images. This does not replace the contact-coordinate tests, and it does not claim that a current twelve-resident or every-coat/every-board matrix was visually reviewed.

## One optional close-zoom refinement

The exposed square cut end of the ridge cap, above the right carton bank where it meets the far rafter, is the one small trim location that could use more explicit construction detail. A localized end-grain treatment and a narrow darker bearing seam would distinguish the cut end from the long-grain faces and make the supported joint easier to parse under close zoom. It already touches the rafter; this is not a gap correction. I do not expect this single detail to materially change the normal full-room reading, so it is not a condition for the preview.

No additional broad contact-shadow pass is recommended from these images. Stronger global edge shading would risk making the clean carton and slate surfaces look outlined again. If further polishing is pursued, keep it local to genuinely ambiguous joins rather than increasing contrast everywhere.

## Limits and prior-review correction

An earlier review accepted the saltbox too broadly on the strength of local masking/contact checks. The user correctly identified that those checks had not established a plausible whole-building roof or height. This review explicitly separates the full-frame roof/tower judgement, current ridge-end evidence, resident images and material comparison. It does not turn green tests into an art-quality claim.

The earlier far-rafter ray finding was analytic; the sampled far-rafter pixel did not establish a before/after raster failure. The collar did have a demonstrated raster failure. Nothing in this final report broadens those earlier claims.
